Spring skiing: where to ski in March and April

For March and April, choose a resort with a lot of terrain above 2,000 to 2,500 metres, ideally with glacier skiing, and start your day early while the snow is firm. Spring snow is a melt-freeze cycle, so firm mornings and soft afternoons are normal. This guide lists high areas that usually run into late April or May.

Updated: 2026-11-20

Spring skiing rewards the right choices and punishes the wrong ones. The same resort can give you firm, fast corduroy at nine and slush at two, or a closed lower mountain by mid-April. Where you go matters, but so does the hour you start and the side of the mountain you ski. This guide covers what spring snow does, which kinds of resorts hold it, and a short sourced list of areas that usually run into April or later.

What spring snow actually does

Spring conditions come from a melt-freeze cycle. After a clear night the surface firms up, and as the day warms it softens into what skiers call corn snow, formed by repeated melting and refreezing.[1] Early in the day the snow is firmer and easier to control, especially at altitude. By afternoon it gets heavy, and the closer you get to April, the more an early start matters.[1]

That has a practical result. Spring is not one condition, it is three: firm in the morning, good in the middle, wet and tiring late. Plan the day around that curve rather than around the lift opening time alone.

What to look for in a spring resort

Three things decide whether a resort still works in March and April.

  • Altitude. Altitude matters more in April than in midwinter. Resorts with a lot of terrain above roughly 2,000 to 2,500 metres benefit from overnight refreezes, while lower slopes soften fast and some resorts close by mid-month.[1]
  • Aspect. South-facing slopes warm first and can turn heavy in the afternoon. North-facing terrain keeps its quality longer.[1]
  • Glacier or high top station. Glacier areas add security because of their height and deep snowpack.[1] They are also where the long season dates come from.

Season dates change every year with snow and operator decisions, so treat every closing month below as typical, not promised. Check the resort's own operating calendar before you book.

Austria

Sölden skis on two glacier areas up to 3,340 metres, and its general season runs from early October to early May.[2] The high top station is what keeps the upper mountain reliable when the valley is warm. Hintertux sits above the Zillertal and describes skiing almost all year round, with lifts reaching slopes under the Olperer; the glacier also has snow cannons.[6] It is a smaller area, so it suits a few good morning hours more than a full week of variety.

France

The Three Valleys are the largest linked area in this list, with 600 kilometres of slopes between 1,100 and 3,230 metres and a general season of mid-November to early May.[3] The size is useful in spring because you can move to the sectors that hold the best snow that day. One mountain guide to April skiing notes that Val Thorens, in the Three Valleys, has most of its terrain above 2,000 metres and that north-facing high sectors hold snow after midday.[1] Tignes and Val d'Isère reach 3,456 metres, partly on glaciers, with a general season from late November to early May.[4] Les 2 Alpes tops out at 3,523 metres, but its general winter season is listed as early December to late April, a little earlier than the others.[5]

Switzerland and Italy

The Zermatt and Cervinia area runs from 1,562 to 3,899 metres with 322 kilometres of slopes, and the Theodul glacier hosts summer skiing.[7] It crosses the Swiss-Italian border, so check both operators when you plan.

Why low and mid-height resorts finish earlier

Not every good resort is a spring resort. KitzSki in Austria has a top at 2,000 metres, and its general season runs from early November to mid-April.[8] That is a fine season, but it ends earlier, and in March the lower slopes can already be soft by midday. This is not a flaw, just a mismatch if your trip falls in the last two weeks of April. If your dates are fixed, pick the resort by altitude first and by village second.

How to time a spring day

  1. Start early. Be on the first lifts while the surface is still firm. The earlier you start, the more of the good window you get.[1]
  2. Follow the sun. Ski east-facing slopes in the morning, then move to slopes that have been in the sun for a while as they soften. Keep north-facing high terrain for after midday.
  3. Go high first, low later. The upper mountain stays firm longer. Lower runs soften sooner, so save them for the middle of the day when they have turned to corn, and check that the valley run is still open before you plan to ski it home.
  4. Stop when it turns heavy. Wet, heavy snow is tiring and a poor time to push on. A long lunch and an early finish beat one more run on tired legs.

Spring days feel easier than midwinter ones but they tire you differently. Wet snow drags at your legs late in the day, so a base of leg strength is worth having before you go. The ski fitness plan is built to get you to the first ski day with trained legs, and ski fitness one week before a trip covers the final stretch.

Pick the dates, then the resort

The earlier in March you go, the less altitude you need. The later in April, the more it counts. For the last weeks of the season, favour a resort with terrain above 2,500 metres and read the operating dates, not just the brochure. Questions

Where is the best place to ski in April?

High resorts and glaciers with terrain above about 2,500 metres are the safest choice, such as Sölden, Val Thorens in the Three Valleys, Tignes or Hintertux. Check each resort's operating dates for the year.

What is corn snow?

Corn snow is spring snow formed by repeated melting and refreezing. It firms up after a clear night and softens as the day warms.

What time should I start skiing in spring?

As early as the lifts open. Mornings are firmer and easier to control, and starting early matters more as April goes on.

Which slopes are best in spring, north or south facing?

South-facing slopes warm first and can get heavy in the afternoon. North-facing terrain keeps its quality longer, so save it for after midday.

Do all ski resorts stay open until May?

No. Lower resorts often close by mid-April, while high or glacier areas typically run to late April or early May. Dates change each year.
