Best time to ski: a month-by-month guide

For reliable snow with lighter crowds, January and early March are the usual picks in the Alps. February has deep snow but the biggest crowds, and April favors high altitude. Colorado peaks in January to March. Choose by what you will trade: snow certainty, quiet or sun.

Updated: 2026-11-20

There is no single best time to ski. There is a best time for what you want: the deepest snow, the quietest lifts, the warmest afternoons or the lowest chance of a closed run. This guide goes month by month, then compares the Alps with North America, and ends with a way to pick your own window.

The short answer

If you want reliable snow and can choose your dates, January and early March are the usual picks in the Alps. January follows the festive rush, snowpack is building and, outside the third week, crowds are lighter. February has deep, solid snow but is the busiest month because of half-term holidays[1][2]. March is quieter after the February rush, with a deep base, though low south-facing slopes can turn slushy in the afternoon[2].

Dates move with each season's snowfall and with school calendars, so treat this as a typical pattern, not a promise.

The pattern comes from a resort guide that describes the European season as typically running from late November to late April, with February the busiest month and lower resorts often closing by mid-April[2]. The Ski Club of Great Britain says a deep snowpack in January is also what reduces, though never removes, avalanche risk[1].

November

Few resorts open, mainly those with glacier access or high-altitude terrain, and snow is variable[2]. It is quiet, but you ski what is open, not what is on the piste map. If you want to start the year this way, see early-season ski resorts.

December

The first two to three weeks are usually quiet. Then school holidays begin around 20 December and the festive weeks become the busiest and most expensive of the winter[2]. The Ski Club notes that conditions often have stabilised by this time and good snow cover can reach all elevations[1]. Early December suits skiers who want space and are happy with a thinner base.

January

Crowds drop once the first week is over. The Ski Club warns that peak weeks in mid-January are often some of the busiest non-school-holiday periods, so the very quietest days are early and late in the month[1]. Days are short, with lifts usually closing between 4 and 4:30 pm[2]. That matters for legs too: you get fewer hours but they are cold, firm and good.

February

The snowpack is deep and solid, which is why many people choose February. The trade-off is that half-term holidays across Europe make the middle weeks the busiest of the season[1]. French school holidays are staggered across three zones, which can stretch the busy stretch into early March[2]. If you are tied to school dates, book early and pick a big ski area where queues spread out.

March

After the February rush slopes are markedly quieter[2], although mid-March can include some of the busiest non-school-holiday weeks, and Easter holidays can fall in March or April[1]. Spring is cheaper and warmer, which helps children. The catch is less certainty of snow in lower resorts around Easter[1]. Prefer higher ski areas and plan your skiing for the morning.

April

Skiing concentrates at high altitude, and lower resorts often close by mid-April[2]. Some glacier areas keep going much longer; a handful of resorts still aim to offer summer skiing for around six weeks each spring and summer, though the Ski Club says this has been drastically affected by climate change[1]. Choose April for sun and calm slopes, not for guaranteed powder.

Alps versus North America

The Alps and the Rockies share the same shape of season, with a different timing and crowd rhythm.

  • Season length. The European season typically runs from late November to late April[2]. Colorado's ski season runs from November through May according to Vail Resorts, with only some resorts, such as Breckenridge, often staying open into May[3].
  • Snow. In Colorado, January and February bring consistent storms, cold temperatures and excellent snow quality, and March is often the snowiest month at several resorts[3]. In the Alps, January and February are described as the reliable months, with March deep but softer[2].
  • Crowds. Christmas and New Year are busy on both continents. In Colorado, January and February are also the busiest time of year, and spring break keeps slopes lively in March and April[3]. In Europe the extra pressure comes from February half-terms, so the quietest Alpine weeks are not the quietest Colorado weeks.

The practical difference is the calendar of holidays. Check the school and public holidays for the country you are skiing in, not just your own.

Snow reliability: what to check

Month matters less than altitude and a few other things. Weather-to-ski, a UK snow-conditions site, notes that height is not the whole story: glacier access, snowmaking, local climate and the amount of terrain a resort plans to open all count[4]. Lower resorts hold snow less reliably, especially at the edges of the season[4].

  1. Altitude of the terrain you will actually ski, not of the village.
  2. Aspect. North-facing slopes keep snow longer; south-facing slopes soften in afternoon sun.
  3. Snowmaking. It helps in lean years but is not infallible, since it needs cold or dry conditions[4].
  4. The current report. Conditions change quickly, so check snow reports close to your trip[4].

How to choose your own window

  1. Start with your constraints. School holidays, work leave and who is coming. These remove most of the calendar for you.
  2. Decide what you will trade. Snow certainty costs you quiet, quiet costs you snow certainty, and sun costs you some snow quality.
  3. Pick one protected week and one flexible fallback. A fallback week or weekend rescues the trip when a storm or a warm spell hits.
  4. Book flexible terms where you can. The cheapest dates are rarely the most flexible ones.
  5. Count your total days. One long week is a different season from five long weekends. See how many ski days per season to set a realistic number.

Beginners and families

For a first trip, easy runs matter more than the deepest snow. The Ski Club calls the Easter-holiday period in March perfect for getting children into skiing, since warmer weather means less chance of children becoming cold and uncomfortable[1]. See beginner first ski trip for the rest of the planning. Very early season is a poor choice for beginners because the easiest runs usually open last; the same is true in Colorado, where Arapahoe Basin's own opening-day notes say its easiest runs take a few additional weeks to open[5].

Whatever month you pick, arrive fit

The month you choose decides your snow. Your legs decide how much of it you enjoy. A quiet month with short lift queues still means long days on your legs, so preparation matters in every month. Questions

What is the best month to ski in the Alps?

January and early March are the usual compromise between reliable snow and lighter crowds. February has deep snow but is the busiest month because of half-term holidays.

When is the cheapest time to ski?

Weeks that avoid school holidays are generally cheaper. That means early December, parts of January, and spring, though spring snow is less certain at lower resorts.

Is March a good month to ski?

Yes, especially at higher resorts. Slopes are quieter after February and the base is deep, but south-facing and low slopes can go soft in the afternoon.

Is it better to ski in Europe or North America?

Neither is better overall. Both have reliable snow from January to February. The difference is holiday timing: Europe peaks at February half-terms, Colorado in January and February and at spring break.

How early should I book a ski trip?

Peak weeks such as Christmas, New Year and February half-terms fill first, so book those early. Off-peak weeks leave more room to wait and check the forecast.

How long before the season should I start training?

About six weeks gives time for three short sessions a week. Count back from your first ski day and start then.
