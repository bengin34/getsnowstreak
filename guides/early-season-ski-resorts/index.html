Early-season ski resorts: where to ski in November and early December

In November your options are glacier areas and a few very high resorts, such as Hintertux, Sölden, Saas-Fee and Tignes, plus early openers in Colorado. Expect limited terrain, hard snow and quiet slopes. Most other Alpine resorts open in December.

Updated: 2026-11-20

Most Alpine resorts do not open until December. If you want to ski in November, or in the first weeks of December, your options are glacier areas, a few very high resorts and a handful of North American ski areas that open early. This guide names them, shows what each one offers, and sets out what to expect once you get there.

What early-season skiing really is

Before mid-November, skiing in the Alps is largely confined to the glaciers, and the majority of Alpine resorts do not open until December[1]. Some stay closed until mid-December or Christmas week whatever the snow cover[1]. Even where a resort opens, it opens a slice of its terrain, not the whole map. Expect fewer lifts, fewer runs and a lot of the same few pistes.

Altitude is the main filter, but not the only one: glacier access, snowmaking, local climate and the amount of terrain a resort plans to open all count[1]. Check how many pistes are actually open, because the resort with the most open terrain is not always the obvious one[1].

Austria

Hintertux is described as the only resort in Austria where the glacier operates year-round, with wide groomed runs and a small, low-key village. Weekend mornings bring day-trippers from Innsbruck, so midweek is quieter[2]. Sölden's World Cup opening races in late October traditionally kick off the European season, and skiing continues through November on its Rettenbach and Tiefenbach glaciers[2]. Stubai is described as the largest glacier ski area in Austria, with wide blue and red runs suited to intermediates[2]. Kitzsteinhorn, near Kaprun, lists its summit at 3,029 m and describes skiing from autumn until early summer[3]. OnTheSnow also lists Pitztal, Mölltal and the Dachstein glacier as autumn options[4].

Obergurgl is typically the first major non-glacial resort to open, around mid-November[1].

Switzerland

Saas-Fee has groomed glacier piste between about 2,500 and 3,500 m in November, with wide, gently graded runs that suit intermediates[2]. Zermatt reaches the Klein Matterhorn glacier, described as the highest lift-served point in Europe[2]. Weather to Ski names Zermatt and Saas-Fee as the main October options in Switzerland[1].

France and Italy

Tignes has glacier skiing on the Grande Motte, which WeSki says typically opens from October, with the main lifts usually opening in late November. Val Thorens typically opens in mid to late November and relies on altitude and snowmaking[2]. Val d'Isère and Ischgl usually follow by late November[1]. Cervinia typically opens in late October or early November, with broad, forgiving pistes[2].

Early-season options in North America

Arapahoe Basin, Copper Mountain, Breckenridge, Keystone and Loveland in Colorado open in October if conditions allow[4]. A-Basin is a good example of how thin the start can be: on its 2024 opening day (2 November) only one run was open, an intermediate, and the resort said its easiest runs take a few additional weeks to open[5]. In Canada, Norquay, Sunshine Village and Lake Louise are usually the first to open, sometimes around Halloween[4].

What to expect on the snow

  • Limited terrain. One or two lifts, a few runs, queues on the open ones.
  • Harder, denser snow. Man-made snow is generally denser and harder than natural snow[1].
  • Ice, not slush. Early-season ice is at its worst when heavy skier traffic sits on a thin base, and steep north-facing slopes are especially prone[1].
  • Variable snow. Snowmaking is not infallible, and a persistently mild early winter can limit it[1].
  • Weak sun. Air temperature, humidity and rain matter more than sunshine for snow quality[1].

Tips for a first trip of the season

  1. Book flexibly or late. Opening dates are targets, not promises. Check current snow reports close to travel, since conditions change quickly[1].
  2. Favor high terrain. Snow at lower altitudes sticks less reliably in November[1].
  3. Choose gentle slopes. Quieter, lower-angle pistes are better when ice is a concern; consistent snowfall is the best cure for early-season ice[1].
  4. Go midweek where you can. Weekend mornings can fill glacier car parks[2].
  5. Keep the plan short. Two or three easy hours a day is a better first outing than six hard ones.
  6. Treat it as a warm-up, not as your main trip. If you want deep snow and full terrain, compare months in best time to ski.

Is early season right for you?

It suits confident intermediates and above who want to shake off the winter break and do not mind a small map. It is a weak choice for beginners, because easy runs are usually the last to open. First-timers should read beginner first ski trip and wait for a fuller season. Costs differ too, but a short trip still counts toward your season, so see how many ski days per season to plan the total.

Early skiing means training earlier

If you ski in November, your training window starts in September, not in autumn. Work back about six weeks from the first day: a trip in the third week of November means starting in early October at the latest. Skiing hard on thin, icy terrain without prepared legs is a poor way to start a season. Questions

Which ski resorts are open in November?

Mainly glacier and very high resorts, such as Hintertux, Sölden, Stubai, Saas-Fee, Zermatt, Tignes and Cervinia. Obergurgl and Val Thorens usually follow in mid to late November.

Can you ski in the Alps in October?

Yes, on glaciers. Weather to Ski names Zermatt, Saas-Fee and Hintertux as the main options. Terrain is limited and conditions depend on the weather.

Is early-season skiing good for beginners?

Usually not. The easiest runs tend to open last because they need more snow, and early-season ice is harder on new skiers.

Where can I ski in North America in early season?

Colorado areas such as Arapahoe Basin, Loveland, Keystone, Copper and Breckenridge open in October if conditions allow. In Canada, Norquay, Sunshine Village and Lake Louise often open first.

How much terrain is open in early season?

Often a fraction of the map. Glacier areas typically list tens of kilometres in November, and a resort may open one or two lifts on opening day.

When should I start training for a November ski trip?

About six weeks before your first day. For a mid-November trip that means starting in early October at the latest.
